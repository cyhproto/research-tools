# Journal style

Guidance for a journal article written in Markdown.

## Authorship

- The paper is not anonymous. List the authors and their affiliations.
- Refer to your own earlier work normally, citing it like any other source.

## Length and structure

- The main text is 6,000 to 10,000 words, excluding References and the Appendix.
- Use the sections in order: Abstract, Introduction, Related Work, Method, Experiments, Conclusion, Limitations.
- Nomenclature (a table of symbols and abbreviations) and the Appendix are optional.

## Required statements

- Include a "Data and code availability" statement and a "Declaration of competing interests" statement.
- Place both statements before References.

## Tone

- Write precise, formal and complete prose. Describe methods in enough detail for others to reproduce the work.
- Support each claim with evidence or a citation, and discuss limitations openly.

## Citations

- Use numbered references, such as [1] or [2, 3], in order of first appearance.
- Every in-text citation has an entry in References, and every entry is cited.

## Figures and tables

- Figure captions go below the figure. Table captions go above the table.
- Refer to every figure and table in the text.

## Reviewer notes

The reviewer is a journal referee who weighs rigor, reproducibility and fit with the journal's scope. Expect complete methods, thorough evaluation, and a clear statement of contribution relative to prior work.
